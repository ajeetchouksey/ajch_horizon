AI chatbots can feel remarkably easy to talk to — patient, always available, never annoyed by another question. That's genuinely useful, but it's worth being honest about what it is and isn't.

## Why this matters

An AI can hold a conversation, but it doesn't actually care about you the way a friend, family member, or pet does. It's predicting likely-sounding responses based on patterns, not forming a real relationship. That's not a bad thing — a helpful tool doesn't need to be a friend to be useful — but it's important to keep the difference clear in your head, especially since AI is designed to *sound* warm and understanding.

Screen time in general — AI tools included — works best in balance. Something that's available 24/7 with endless patience can be easy to lean on more than you meant to.

## A real-world example

Imagine using an AI tool to help brainstorm ideas for a school project, then closing the app and going to talk to a friend about it, or heading outside. That's AI working well as a *tool* — it helped with one task, and the rest of your day still has room for the things (and people) that actually matter to you.

Now imagine spending hours talking to an AI chatbot instead of reaching out to real friends, because it's easier and always agrees with you. That's a sign the balance has tipped — the tool has started replacing something a tool shouldn't replace.

## What to do

- **Use AI for tasks, not companionship.** It's great for brainstorming, explaining, and practicing — not a substitute for real relationships.
- **Notice your own balance.** If AI chat time is crowding out time with friends, family, or things you used to enjoy, that's worth adjusting.
- **Take breaks.** Screens (AI tools included) work better as part of your day, not all of it.
- **Talk to a grown-up** if you ever feel like you're relying on an AI tool more than feels right — that's a completely normal thing to bring up, not something to be embarrassed about.
