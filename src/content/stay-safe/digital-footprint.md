Your "digital footprint" is the trail of information you leave behind every time you use the internet — what you post, what you search, what you type into an app, even what you click. Unlike footprints in sand, this trail doesn't always wash away.

## Why this matters

A lot of what you type online — including into AI chat tools — can be stored, sometimes for a long time. Even things that feel private or temporary, like a message in a game chat or a question you asked an AI, might not actually disappear when you close the app. And once something is posted publicly, other people can save, screenshot, or share it beyond your control, even if you delete it later.

This connects directly back to privacy and data: your digital footprint is basically the shape of all the data you've left behind, put together.

## A real-world example

Imagine posting something in the heat of the moment — an angry comment, an embarrassing photo, a joke that doesn't land the way you meant it. In person, that moment passes. Online, a screenshot of it can outlast the moment by years, and show up again in a context you never expected — like someone finding it much later and judging you by something you posted when you were much younger.

The same caution applies to AI tools: if you wouldn't want a message printed out and shown to your future self, it's worth thinking twice before typing it in.

## What to do

- **Think before you share** — not just posts, but comments, chat messages, and anything you type into an AI tool.
- **Assume it could last.** Treat anything you put online as if it might still be around years from now.
- **Use privacy settings** where they exist, and ask a grown-up for help understanding what a setting actually does before changing it.
- **Remember deleting isn't the same as disappearing** — if someone already saw or saved something, deleting it afterward doesn't undo that.
