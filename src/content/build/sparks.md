Two beginner-friendly projects for Sparks (ages 8–10) — no coding required, just curiosity and (for both of these) a grown-up nearby since they involve using an AI chat or image tool.

## Project 1: Talk to an AI Storyteller

Practice asking an AI good questions by co-writing a short story with it.

**What you need:** an AI chat tool a grown-up has approved for you, and a grown-up sitting with you.

**Steps:**
1. Pick three things together: a main character, a place, and a problem to solve (for example: *a curious fox, a floating island, a missing key*).
2. Ask the AI to start a short story using your three things — just the first paragraph.
3. Read what it wrote out loud. Decide together: what happens next?
4. Ask the AI to continue the story your way — give it a specific instruction, like "have the fox find a hidden door."
5. Keep going for 4–5 rounds until you reach an ending.

**What you're learning:** how to give an AI clear, specific instructions (a "prompt") instead of vague ones — notice how a specific instruction like "have the fox find a hidden door" gets a much better result than just saying "continue."

**Remember:** never type your real name, address, or school into the chat — use a made-up character name instead, per Golden Rule #1.

## Project 2: Make an AI Coloring Buddy

Use an AI image tool to generate a simple line-drawing you can print and color in.

**What you need:** an AI image tool a grown-up has approved, a printer (optional — you can also just look at it on screen), and crayons or markers.

**Steps:**
1. Think of something you'd love to color — an animal, a spaceship, a castle.
2. Describe it out loud to your grown-up: what is it doing? What's around it?
3. Together, turn that into a prompt, like "a simple black-and-white line drawing of a dragon reading a book, for coloring in."
4. Generate the image and look at it together — did it match what you imagined?
5. If not, try changing one detail in your prompt and generate again.
6. Print (or screenshot) your favorite version and color it in!

**What you're learning:** that AI image tools respond to *how* you describe something — and that trying again with a small change is a normal, useful part of working with AI, not a failure.
