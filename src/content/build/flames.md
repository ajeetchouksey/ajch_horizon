Two hands-on projects for Flames (ages 11–13) — a Scratch-based chatbot project, and a responsible-creation exercise with AI art.

## Project 1: Build a Simple Chatbot in Scratch

Scratch (the free block-based coding tool at scratch.mit.edu) has AI extension blocks that let you build a basic chatbot without writing traditional code.

**What you need:** a free Scratch account (a grown-up should help set this up), and about 30–45 minutes.

**Steps:**
1. Start a new Scratch project and pick a sprite (character) to be your chatbot.
2. Use an "ask and wait" block so your chatbot can ask the user a question and wait for typed input.
3. Use "if / else" blocks to check what the user typed — for example, if they typed "hello," make your sprite say "Hi there!"
4. Add at least 5 different questions-and-responses so your chatbot can handle a short conversation (a greeting, a "how are you," a joke request, a goodbye, and one you invent).
5. Add a fallback response for when the input doesn't match anything you planned for — like "I'm not sure how to respond to that yet!"
6. Test it with a friend or family member — did it break on anything unexpected?

**What you're learning:** the "if / else" pattern is the same basic idea real chatbots use to decide how to respond — and you'll quickly discover why a fallback response matters, since you can never predict everything someone might type.

## Project 2: Generate Art Responsibly

Use an AI image tool to create original artwork — and practice the habits that make that use *responsible*, not just possible.

**What you need:** an AI image tool a grown-up has approved.

**Steps:**
1. Sketch or describe an original idea first — before touching the AI tool. This keeps *you* as the creative source, with AI as a helper.
2. Turn your idea into a clear, detailed prompt (style, mood, colors, what's happening).
3. Generate a few versions and pick your favorite — or combine ideas from a couple of them.
4. Write one sentence describing what you changed or added yourself (a caption, a title, editing it further) — this is what makes it *your* piece, not just the AI's output.
5. If you ever share AI-generated art, label it as AI-assisted. That's part of being honest about your work, per Golden Rule #5.

**What you're learning:** how to use AI as a creative *tool* while keeping your own ideas and judgment in the driver's seat — and why labeling AI-assisted work honestly matters, especially as these tools get more common.
