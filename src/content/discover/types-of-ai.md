"AI" isn't one single thing — it's a label for a bunch of different tools that all use the same basic pattern-guessing trick, just aimed at different jobs. Here's how to tell the main kinds apart.

## Chatbots (text in, text out)

These take the words you type and predict a helpful, relevant reply — one word-pattern at a time. They're trained on huge amounts of text, so they've learned the patterns of how humans usually write and answer questions. They're great for explaining things or brainstorming, but remember: they're guessing a *likely-sounding* answer, not looking up a guaranteed-true fact.

## Image generators (words in, pictures out)

These turn a text description into a picture by learning patterns from millions of existing images and their captions. Ask for "a dragon riding a skateboard," and it combines patterns it has seen for "dragon," "skateboard," and "riding" into something new. It's genuinely creative-*looking*, but it's still pattern-recombination, not imagination the way you'd experience it.

## Recommenders (your history in, a suggestion out)

These look at patterns in what you (and people like you) have liked before, and guess what you'll want next. Every "up next" video, "you might also like" product, or suggested song comes from this kind of AI. You already saw this one in "AI in your day."

## Robots and game bots (sensors in, actions out)

These take in information about the world — a camera feed, a game state — and use learned patterns to decide what to do next: turn left, dodge, pick up the object. This is the kind of AI behind self-driving cars, robot vacuums, and non-player characters in video games.

## Why the categories matter

Knowing which *kind* of AI you're dealing with helps you know what to expect from it — and what not to trust it for. A chatbot is not a search engine (it can sound sure of something that's wrong). An image generator is not a camera (it can't show you a real photo of something that never happened). Matching the right expectations to the right tool is a big part of using AI wisely instead of blindly.
