AI can feel impressive enough that it's tempting to think it's basically a person. It's worth being really clear about where AI is genuinely strong — and where it isn't even in the same category as a human.

## What AI is great at

- **Spotting patterns in huge amounts of data**, way faster than any human could.
- **Never getting tired or bored** — it'll process the millionth example exactly as carefully as the first.
- **Being available any time**, with endless patience for repeated questions.
- **Doing the same task the exact same way**, over and over, without the small mistakes tiredness causes in people.

## What only humans can do

- **Actually feel things.** An AI can write a very convincing sad story, but it isn't sad. It's predicting what sad-sounding words tend to come next based on patterns, not experiencing anything.
- **Make real ethical judgment calls.** AI can be trained to follow rules about what's fair or harmful, but it doesn't *understand* why fairness matters the way a person who has experienced unfairness does.
- **Truly understand meaning and context**, especially the messy, changing, human kind — sarcasm, culture, "reading the room."
- **Take responsibility.** If an AI gives bad advice, it doesn't feel guilty or learn a lesson the way a person does. A human still has to be accountable for how it's used.

## A concrete example

Imagine an AI and a human doctor both looking at an X-ray. The AI might spot a pattern in the image faster and more consistently than the doctor — that's a real strength. But the AI doesn't know the patient is scared, doesn't notice they're avoiding eye contact because they're hiding something relevant, and can't make the judgment call of "this technically matches the pattern, but given everything else about this specific person, I should double-check before saying anything." A human doctor combines the pattern-matching *and* the human understanding. The AI, alone, only has the first part.

## Why this difference matters

This is exactly why Spark's Golden Rule says **AI is a helper, not the boss.** AI can be a genuinely useful tool for the pattern-spotting part of a task. But the thinking, feeling, and deciding — the part that actually matters most — stays with you.

> **Quick check:** Can you think of a task where you'd trust an AI's pattern-spotting completely, and one where you'd never let an AI make the final call on its own? What's different about the two?
